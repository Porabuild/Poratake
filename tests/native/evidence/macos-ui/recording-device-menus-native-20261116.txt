macOS isolated recording device menus, 2026-11-16

The GPUI native UI probe used an isolated configuration and generated preview,
at normal AppKit window level, dark appearance, and 2x physical backing scale.
A fixture daemon returned generated camera names and no iOS devices. Neither
camera nor microphone hardware, system audio, nor screen capture was started.
Window images were obtained by the already-authorized computer-use helper's
ScreenCaptureKit window capture, not by the Poratake process.

The camera menu opened in a centered 332x300-point window. Its accessibility
menu exposed Camera, System Default (Built-in camera), Built-in camera, and
USB camera with a deliberately long device name; the latter was visually
ellipsized. On dismissal the window returned to 276x44 points, its toolbar was
276 points wide, and an AppKit hit test four points outside reached another
window. The camera menu was opened with a synthetic GPUI pointer event; no
camera item was activated because selecting it could request camera access.

The iOS menu opened in the same 332x300-point window and showed None. Selecting
None through the helper's background accessibility action was confirmed; the
menu closed and the window returned to 276x44 points. A second iOS open/dismiss
also returned to that frame and passed the outside hit test. The fixture daemon
logged only media-devices.list with kinds [camera] and recording-control.listIOSDevices.

The helper initially returned a window image where only the None menu was visible,
with the toolbar area transparent/black; another returned a blank image. After
separate synthetic pointer exit and entry, a further helper window capture showed
both toolbar and menu. The accessibility tree retained toolbar controls. The
partial and full captures are retained; they do not distinguish a helper capture
artifact from an actual compositor or redraw issue. Physical visual confirmation
at the production floating window level is still needed.

The initial probe's hit-test assertion ran before AppKit completed the resize
following menu dismissal and the isolated process aborted. A second run waited
for the observed frame change before testing and exited normally. The existing
editor and its unsaved work were not touched.

Follow-up comparison, 2026-11-16

On a fresh isolated run, four consecutive helper captures of the open iOS menu
contained the toolbar. After dismissing and reopening the menu, four more helper
captures also contained it. The captures were 664x600 pixels; the same 552x88
pixel toolbar region contained 7,338 pixels above 20% RGB brightness in each.
This metric was used only to establish that the toolbar was present, not as a
pixel parity requirement.

In a separate run, record-scene-snapshot rendered GPUI's most recently painted
scene to a Metal-backed RGBA image without capturing the screen. The helper then
captured the same native window using its own already-granted Screen Recording
access. Both retained images show the toolbar and None menu. Each had 7,338
bright pixels in the same toolbar region. Compared over black, the images had a
mean RGB difference of 0.24 levels; 58,211 of 1,195,200 channels differed by
more than two, with maximum 21.82. The result does not explain the earlier
partial or blank helper captures and does not prove what was visible on screen
at the time. No Poratake screen capture or device access was used.
