macOS GPUI recording preview, generated H.264 checkerboard

The parity development executable opened the generated video with its normal
decoder and rendered the preview with GPUI's offscreen macOS renderer. A
scratch profile and fixture daemon were used; no display pixels were captured.
The pointer was simulated. The preview was checked before hover, with hover
controls open, and after exit. It decoded a sharp thumbnail at rest, a blurred
thumbnail while hovered, and a sharp thumbnail again after exit.

Mean horizontal RGB edge differences in a 58 x 63 logical-pixel region away
from controls, in idle / hover / exit order:
  dark 100%: 5.403 / 0.017 / 5.403
  light 125%: 5.425 / 0.017 / 5.425
  dark 200%: 5.369 / 0.017 / 5.369
  light 200%: 5.369 / 0.017 / 5.369

The Electron 43 preview component was rendered in a hidden offscreen window
with the same generated video at 200%. Its video decoded (readyState 4), but
the development fixture did not observe a loadeddata event and the loading
placeholder stayed in the DOM. Applying scale-105 on hover raised that
placeholder above the video and produced the dark no-ready image. Dispatching
a synthetic loadeddata event removed the placeholder and produced the ready
image. This does not establish whether the missing event occurs in the normal
Electron app or whether its physical window paints identically.

With that synthetic event, the dark and light hover images differed from GPUI
by mean 3.916 and 3.951 RGB levels respectively, maximum 6, over the same
116 x 126 physical-pixel region. GPUI's four-point margin was removed before
comparison. The region excludes controls and rounded edges. The reference
includes Chromium video decoding and CSS blur; the GPUI path decodes, downsizes,
blurs and then scales its thumbnail. The images are not pixel-identical.

This evidence does not cover a physical pointer, actual desktop compositing,
live recording, capture exclusion, or other displays.
