macOS GPUI redaction stress and canvas region read, 2026-11-16

The deterministic generated matrix uses 48 cases and four output sizes. It
contains 46 blur and 43 pixelation effects, all ten intensities, 60 clipped
rectangles, 68 reverse-dragged rectangles and ten overlapping effects. Selected
cases include zoom, a generated camera image and annotations before and after
the effects. The extended matrix uses three authoring-canvas sizes and checks
every output pixel: 99,258 pixels in affected rectangles, including boundary
pixels, match a resized full composition within one channel level; the other
847,734 match both the unmodified scaled composition and a preview without
redactions exactly. A separate generated test checks composed pixels at segment
start and end times, including two active effects at the same instant. This is
generated CPU-rendered content, not a real recording or a physical display
comparison.

The shared Canvas read_region routine now copies the in-bounds part of each row
at once and leaves out-of-bounds padding transparent. A native test checks
ordinary reads, all sides of the canvas, entirely off-canvas reads, extreme
signed coordinates and zero-width rejection. Screenshot preview, annotation,
canvas and video composition suites passed after the change.

An ignored same-process benchmark alternates the old per-pixel routine and the
new row-copy routine on an 800x600 region from a synthetic 4K frame. It checks
their bytes match, discards the first two of 16 measurements and reports the
upper median. Two runs gave 2.154 and 2.021 ms for the old routine, versus
0.275 and 0.254 ms for the new one. This measures copying only. Full-preview
timings varied substantially with machine load, so these results do not
establish a playback frame rate or a comparable end-to-end speedup.

Before the row-copy change, the synthetic quality-size probe measured a median
9.28 ms for a 640x360 preview of a 4K frame with an 800x600 pixelation region.
The editor uses half a frame interval as its adaptive composition budget,
about 8.33 ms at 60 fps. The probe excludes decode, upload and scheduling; it
does not establish what happens in live playback. Native playback and slower
machines still require profiling.
