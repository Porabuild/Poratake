macOS GPUI oversized screenshot texture sampling, 2026-11-17, source 0e88eb06

The first oversized-image tiling fix was checked with a high-detail generated
image and an untiled crop of the same pixels. In dark appearance at simulated
1x and 25% zoom, the opaque 17,000x128 tiled image differed from its 512x128
reference in 1,176 of 3,472 interior display pixels by more than two RGB levels;
the maximum was 95. The sampled region crossed the first tile seam. Both images
were rendered by the GPUI Metal fixture after simulated panning.

The macOS plain-image tile renderer now uses the complete image's snapped
destination bounds to place and sample every tile. Its overlap covers a rounded
tile boundary at the supported minimum zoom. Tiles outside the current content
mask are skipped. The original image remains available for export and effects.
The non-macOS plain-image tile path retains its preceding behavior.

For the follow-up, the source was 17,024x128 or 128x17,024. This size makes
the full image and reference crop land on the same integer device-pixel extent
at the tested zooms and simulated scales. Without that condition, independently
rounding a large image and a small crop changes their effective scales and the
comparison does not isolate tile sampling. The 512-pixel reference crosses the
first tile seam. It contains detailed opaque or varying-alpha generated pixels.

Both orientations, both alpha variants, 25% and 50% zoom, simulated display
scales 1, 1.25 and 2, and light and dark appearances passed. Across each
interior comparison, no pixel differed by more than two RGB levels and the
largest observed channel difference was one. This is a generated Metal preview
comparison, not a decoded export or physical-display test.
